### 4. Direct developer interview

https://www.themanequest.com/blog/2023/3/29/we-wanted-to-build-a-horse-game-that-people-could-be-passionate-about-the-inspirations-research-and-future-plans-behind-star-equestrian — accessed 12:12 UTC.

Foxie’s product manager explained the deliberate focus on specific horse animation, separate gait controls, free camera, and optional care. Feeding advances horses and can be performed through a menu or physically for roleplay. Repetitive compulsory brushing/hoof-picking was deliberately avoided. Their three intended playstyles were adventure/story, competitive eventing, and player-organized social trail rides. This supports prioritizing the feel of the horse and player freedom over an ever-growing compulsory checklist.

Re-read 12:54–12:56 UTC: the developer reports that beta players asked for features already present, exposing tutorialization problems. Snowdrop was deliberately easier to control than the later starter horse, with extra riding missions added to communicate the difference. Horse levels affected acceleration, speed and turning. This is historical design intent, not a measurement of current handling. It reinforces two priorities: make existing features discoverable, and let a new player experience satisfying riding before asking them to invest in progression.

## Meadowlark baseline code comparison

Initial read-only source: first `the original checkout`, then the parent’s complete checkout `the isolated implementation checkout`. These findings describe the baseline; the guidance and event-preparation fixes are recorded separately below.

- `story-quests.js` already supplies a prologue, naming, loss of a mare, later search and qualification tracks, NPCs, regional chapters and reward claims. The issue cannot be described accurately as “needs quests.” Assess whether each instruction leads a new rider to an obvious world action and whether the scenes are staged convincingly.
- `se-journey.js` already builds a broad journey dashboard from live progression. A dashboard offers overview; it does not substitute for a clear next action on the riding screen.
- `events-pvp.js` already has tickets, ranks, hazards, race items and “favoured traits.” However its `traitMatches` checks numeric stats against a threshold, and its race multiplier boosts pace based on those stats. This differs from SE’s described personality/trait matching and qualitative course privileges. Avoid claiming feature parity from shared labels.
- `events2-ladder.js` already has event cards, requirements, reward/results progression and local competitor fields. Prioritize helping the player choose an appropriate horse and understand the next improvement, rather than adding another event menu. A further 12:57 UTC inspection confirms its consolidated result card already carries racing points gained, rank progress and reward claims, a discipline score sheet and retry. Assess the actual flow before proposing another results overlay.
- `course-guide.js` already has ground chevrons, target rings, jumping-time feedback and course cameras. Test it in the actual first course before proposing duplicate navigation code.
- `se-hud.js:415` resolves objectives for doors, explicit visits, talk targets and clue witnesses. Other unfinished types fall back to the quest giver. Consequently a forage, gallop or event task can point back at Wren rather than to its world action. This is a concrete target-resolution gap, not a claim that all navigation is missing.
- `ranch3d.html:1224` already sets all need-decay rates to zero and starts horses with full needs, matching an existing owner request. Do not reintroduce upkeep chores. The remaining care question is presentation and satisfying interaction.
- `breeding.js` already models lineage, genetic coats, breeding tokens, potions, foals and prestige. Its stat aggregation loops over `h.trait` and `h.prestige`, so the exact trait structure is not equivalent to the official newer description of concurrent purebred/crossbreed/prestige types. This is a secondary depth difference, not the most important first-session improvement.
- Existing September research documents claim everything is shipped and that Meadowlark “beats” Star Equestrian based on feature lists. Those conclusions are not supported by direct side-by-side play, mobile performance, animation or onboarding evaluation.
- Event preparation already contains useful real math: `se-events.js` uses effective stats for entry gates, while `events2-disciplines.js` has a trained-plus-tack breakdown and discipline-specific benefits. However the normal screen puts actual values inside hover titles and its Full card route uses the simpler ladder preview. Showing those existing current/required values on touch, and exposing the existing richer card, improves a concrete decision without inventing a cosmetic fit percentage.
- `world.js` already provides tameable herds, carrots, a trust HUD, following, cooperative taming, and a choice to keep or release to sanctuary. Its calm-proximity trust model is different from the observed SE chase/rest/gesture loop. Replacing that established model is a separate design decision; a feature label alone is insufficient evidence of parity.

## Recommended acceptance criteria (inference)

1. A fresh player understands the current objective, where to go and which action to use without opening a large menu.
2. The first useful interaction happens in the world; reward completion clearly tells the player what changed and gives one sensible next step.
3. The horse can walk, trot, canter, stop and jump predictably, with controls sized for thumbs and camera framing that shows the approach.
4. Horse choice has a visible reason: current capability, course demand, personality and training matter in understandable ways.
5. Care produces an appealing physical interaction and a positive bond/training result, without making a returning player immediately repair several depleted meters.
6. Completing a round teaches something: time, faults, reward, personal best and a useful retry/improvement action.
7. App Store intent requires reliability in the complete first-session flow on device. None of the web research proves Meadowlark is release-ready.

## Priorities arising from the study (inference)

1. Finish and observe one complete new-player loop on touch: story setup, world interaction, sustained riding, first event, result, retry and return to exploration. Measure confusion and interruptions, not only successful button calls.
2. Review the approved horse, rider, tack, camera, terrain and lighting together at the same location. Judge motion through transitions and obstacles, not only a static horse screenshot.
3. Improve the connections among existing systems: an objective should lead to its action; preparation should explain readiness; completion should explain progress and the next choice. Do not add duplicate dashboards to compensate for weak connections.
4. Validate performance and save/lifecycle reliability on actual supported devices before treating a native wrapper as a release candidate. The current source and browser inspections cannot establish this.
5. Build trustworthy social infrastructure before a native public launch. The appearance of club pages is separate from reliable identity, progression and moderation.

The strongest conclusion is that a coherent, readable and satisfying session matters more than matching the reference game's number of named features. No exact-parity or App Store-readiness claim follows from this research.
