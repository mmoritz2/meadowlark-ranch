This diagnostic measures 128 actual Three.js Walk poses on the White, Bay and Sporthorse native models before the head-timing repair and on the private Icelandic kit. The measured source files are pinned separately in `measure.mjs`, so changing a runtime profile does not overwrite this baseline. It changes no models or game code. `head_019` and `spine_04_012` world origins are approximate head and upper-trunk landmarks; they are not identical to laboratory optical markers.

The current head landmark spans 32–41 mm vertically and the upper-trunk landmark 15–19 mm. Their second-harmonic phase difference is only 34–35 degrees, equivalent to about 5% of the full stride. The trunk's fourth harmonic is stronger than its second harmonic, consistent with the authored pelvis translation `sin(4*theta)`: four repetitions per full stride. All samples are finite and the browser reported no errors. Full measurements and phase rows are saved in `measurements.json`.

As a qualitative reference, [Loscher et al. (2016)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5136594/) measured eight adult warmbloods walking and reported two head and withers oscillations per stride, with a quarter-stride offset between them. Mean head and thorax vertical spans were 91 and 32 mm. Different landmarks, proportions and speeds prevent treating those values as exact acceptance thresholds for these models, especially the Icelandic conformation. Nevertheless, the nearly synchronous head/trunk cycle and repeated body ripple are concrete targets for improvement.

[Hodson, Clayton and Lanovaz (2000)](https://beva.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2746/042516400777032237) provide additional normal Walk references for the sequence of forelimb flexion and terminal-swing extension. [Lawson and Marlin (2010)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21059059/) describe scapular sliding and rotation; these guide a coupled shoulder/forelimb solve, rather than copying amplitudes into every breed.

Next review separates a bounded head/neck timing experiment from a body/limb solve. Neck-only work can preserve every current limb track and contact. Changing the torso cycle requires solving and checking the feet again; larger arbitrary body waves would not address the timing error.
