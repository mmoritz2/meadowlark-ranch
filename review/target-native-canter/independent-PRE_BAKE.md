# Primary basis for the native Canter pilot

Private scope: original realistic white horse/native rig and Western tack; two leads of a new three-beat Canter pilot. No new model acquisition, production edits or reuse of the source's mislabeled Trot/pace.

## Primary observations

The 2023 overground Canter study distinguishes trailing hind, leading hind plus trailing fore, then leading fore. It measured functional differences between leading/trailing limbs, including more protraction in leading limbs and greater retraction/hip extension in the trailing hind. Thus the second beat must be an opposite-side diagonal pair, with the final forelimb identifying the lead; the gait must not be a mirrored two-beat Trot. [St. George et al., 2023, Electromyographic and Kinematic Comparison](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-2615/13/11/1755).

In elite dressage horses, collected Canter had mean stride duration 0.629 s and only 0.013 s of suspension; pirouettes had no suspension and more diagonal dissociation. This demonstrates that collection cannot be defined simply by slowing a loop or adding an arbitrary long flight. [Burns and Clayton, 1997, Canter pirouette and collected Canter](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9354291/).

A primary three-horse overground study found modest trunk/head/neck pitch, even in Canter; trunk was the most stabilized segment. Neck protraction began late in suspension and continued into fore support; retraction began near leading-fore touchdown and continued into early suspension. The head could counter-rotate relative to neck, instead of being held rigidly to it. Some slow Canters lacked suspension. These support restrained, single-cycle axial response rather than exaggerated rocking or carrying the Trot's two identical body pulses into Canter. [Dunbar et al., 2008, Stabilization and mobility](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2768006/).

The six-horse collected/working/medium/extended Canter comparison found speed changes chiefly through stride length, with stride duration maintained and inter-limb timing changing. Therefore speed is a property of stance backflow and stride displacement, not merely playback speed. [Clayton, 1994, Comparison of Canters](https://beva.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1111/j.2042-3306.1994.tb04866.x).

These small and discipline-specific studies guide gait identity and motion structure. They do not certify this animation's naturalism or prescribe one universal joint/phase waveform. The 2022 head/withers/pelvis timing paper used for Trot did not study Canter and is not used as Canter evidence.

## Agreed bounded authoring choice

- **Left:** RH trailing hind at phase 0; LH + FR middle diagonal at .24; FL leading fore at .48.
- **Right:** LH at 0; RH + FL at .24; FR at .48.
- Period **.64 s**, duty **.40**. Intended flight **.88–1.00**, after the final fore toe-off. This is an explicit 12% pilot suspension, not the measured collected-Canter timing above.
- Stance stroke **±.25 m** over **.256 s**, implying **1.953125 m/s** (~7.03 km/h) and nominal actor travel **1.25 m per cycle**. Label it a slow, short-stride Canter footfall study, not a reproduction of measured collected/working Canter.
- A modest single-cycle torso pitch, total range around **3–4°**, mild **30–40 mm** body Y range, with retained high standing posture and zero lateral roll, are conservative authoring suggestions rather than measured prescriptions. Compress mildly during overlapping/fore support and rise into suspension; avoid persistent lowering to extend reach.
- Neck protracts from late suspension through hind/middle support, then retracts around leading-fore touchdown into early suspension; permit compensating head pitch. Groom motion should follow that gait cycle, not stay static or use unchanged Trot timing.
- Keep dynamic heel/flat/toe orientation and anatomical fore carpus/hind hock flexion. A long joint-cap plateau remains a timing defect even if endpoint heights are perfect. The native left/right rig geometry is not exactly mirrored, so both leads require independent checks.
