# Private left/right Canter footfall study

This passes as a narrow private slow, short-stride Canter study on the original realistic white Western horse. Both lead counterparts are authored separately on the target's upright default pose. Own and independent contact, travel, loop and sampled side/quarter checks pass. It is not a measured collected-canter reproduction, full gait-package approval, or an edit to the live game/roster.

Frozen candidate: `target-native-canter.glb`, SHA-256 `81039eb4d4c2e75ae992b78b4e6382d41bd7b302c5c4fdce75388a71901045d2`. Regenerate using `python3 review/target-native-canter/build.py` with `native-walk-joint-reference.json` beside the saved builder. Open `/review/target-native-canter/audit-viewer.html` and select either lead. The two appended clips are `Target Native Canter Left` and `Target Native Canter Right`.

## Authored timing and motion

Left lead lands RH, then the middle diagonal LH+FR, then LF. Right lead mirrors this as LH, RH+FL, FR. Onsets are 0, 0.24 and 0.48, duty factor is 0.40, and cycle duration is 0.64 seconds. Intended suspension occurs at phases 0.88–1.0. Overlap between adjacent supports is intentional; three beats refer to touchdown events, not a requirement for exactly one/two planted feet at every instant.

Each limb has a 0.50 m stance stroke. Backflow over 0.256 seconds implies **1.953125 m/s** travel. This is an honest low-speed target-reach compromise, not the ~4.4 m/s canter measured in the primary ridden-canters study. The clips are not a retimed Trot: there is one asymmetric three-beat support/body cycle rather than two diagonal Trot cycles. Leading limbs have small forward stroke-center offsets and distinct swing bias. Runtime speed must match the authored backflow.

Pelvis Y delta is `0.008 + 0.018*cos(2*pi*phase)` meters: a 36-mm range, with mild compression during overlapping/leading-fore support and rise toward suspension. Torso pitch has roughly 3–4 degrees total global range. Neck protracts during hind/middle support and retracts after leading-fore touchdown; head global pitch is smaller than neck pitch, providing relative compensation rather than freezing them together. There is no lateral body roll. Original standing height and zero preview root travel are retained.

Hooves use heel-first placement, a flat middle support and toe-anchored late breakover. Swing hoof pitch changes freely and legs fold with explicit fore carpal / hind hock bias. The solver fits the actual whole lower-hoof skin patch minimum plus the active fixed heel, sole or toe centroid. Raised heel vertices are intentional during rollover, not forced flat. Fore swing lift is 15 cm and hind lift 14 cm, with an early flight peak and smooth endpoint easing. Fifty-one native groom detail controls receive a stronger phase-lagged response; the whole tail base adds 4.5-degree yaw and 1.5-degree pitch, with yaw sign corresponding to lead. These are restrained periodic authored controls, not physical hair simulation.

## Independent measured evidence

The independent actual-skin scan samples 256 phases per lead. Left intended stance minima span +0.863–4.297 mm and right +0.830–2.752 mm above the unchanged fixed floor. Complete body minima remain positive. Each lead has 30/256 intended airborne samples (11.72%); actual complete body minima during those intervals range roughly +1.67–48.14 mm. The touchdown/support patterns agree with the separate three-beat lead definitions.

At nominal 1.953125 m/s actor travel, maximum regional active-anchor span is 2.509 mm for left lead and 2.530 mm for right, at the native FL toe centroid during breakover. This residual is larger than the previous Walk/Trot studies and remains disclosed. Contact centroids are proxies rather than exact individual planted vertices, and heel movement after toe lift is not counted as planted-heel slip.

Leading fore swing geometric flexion reaches 79.6 degrees in left lead and 78.1 in right, while trailing fore maxima are 67.5/67.9 degrees. Hind maxima are 83.9/86.8 degrees. These bone-center interior-angle measurements demonstrate distinct leading/trailing articulation, not clinical ROM. Independent first/last orientation seams are below 0.000006 degrees and hoof centroid seams below 0.00004 mm. Loop closure does not certify acceleration smoothness.

Measured upper-trunk excursion is 54.39 mm, neck crest 58.08 mm and head pivot 124.42 mm; head relative to the trunk moves 81.75 mm. The pelvis is lowest at phase 0.5, whereas the upper trunk reaches its minimum at 0.352 and head at 0.266. Neck/body pitching contributes to these different marker phases; the conspicuous head response remains an authoring choice requiring visual review. The independent summaries, regional travel/cap/loop metrics, primary-source basis, two sampled side-cycle GIFs and two selected stills are preserved here. Large raw scans and redundant captures remain in ignored `output/target-native-canter-audit/`.

## Bounded ranges and limitations

Fore rest-relative sagittal bounds are scapula ±18 degrees, upper arm ±38, lower arm ±48, carpus −15/+70. Hind bounds are hip ±38, stifle ±50, hock −50/+20. These model-space controls are not clinical joint ROM. The standing fore chain is nearly straight; scapular motion is included rather than lowering the whole body to force reach.

Grouped bound touches occur in left lead HL 6, FL 48, HR 27, FR 39 of 128 key frames, and right lead HL 19, FL 38, HR 16, FR 49. Maximum local correction is 70 degrees. Per-joint counts are retained in `per-joint-caps.json`: the large fore grouped counts are scapula −18-degree touches (38–49 frames), while leading carpus +70 is reached only 8 left-lead frames (~40 ms) and 13 right-lead frames (~65 ms). Trailing fore carpals do not reach their bounds. Hind hock −50 touches range from 6 to 22 frames; the native right hock also reaches +20 for 4–5 frames near extension. Upper arm, lower arm, hip and stifle caps are not touched. These remaining held-apex/extension phases must remain disclosed. Trajectories retain each hoof’s original default rest-Z center, which is asymmetric by about 0.168 m between fore feet and 0.207 m between hind feet. The two leads mirror footfall roles and recovery bias rather than restructuring those native rest centers; absolute protraction/placement therefore differs between leads. No extra cap widening, rest-center restructuring or repeated tuning is planned for this bounded pass. Floor accuracy alone does not certify naturalistic push-off, acceleration, limb asymmetry, rider fit or lead transitions.

## Provenance and primary basis

Appearance and native rig are preserved from the registered [WildMesh realistic horse demo](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/horse-realistic-3d-model-demo-free-65d6a70a6721495f938c93e80a5998e4), under CC BY-NC 4.0; existing attribution/license applies. This Canter motion is target-authored and uses no new downloads or imported source pace.

The [leading/trailing limb canter study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10252091/) supports the trailing-hind / middle-diagonal / leading-fore sequence and leading/trailing limb differences. Its ridden canters averaged about 4.4 m/s with roughly 0.59-second strides; this candidate's shorter stroke is explicitly different. The [collected canter timing study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9354291/) reports short suspension in its measured collected performance, illustrating that suspension duration varies with performance. [Head, neck and trunk stabilization research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2768006/) supports distinct single-cycle segment pitching and head/neck compensation during canter. These informed qualitative authoring, not numerical certification of this candidate.
